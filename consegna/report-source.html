Why we steered away from mainstream romantasy

Epic romantasy (fae courts, dragon riders) sells the most, but it is also the most crowded. Big publishers lead it, and readers expect novels of 90,000 words or more, so a novella placed there is judged against those books. In the three sub-niches above, short reads are an established, loved format. A well-crafted 15,000-word book can compete there on equal terms, and the series can be published faster.

How we picked them: close to romantasy, indie authors among the bestsellers, novellas accepted, strong Kindle Unlimited readership and readers who follow series.

What this means for your book

  • The demand is real and it sits next to romantasy. Cozy fantasy is described as one of the hottest niches of 2026, and monster romance has moved from indie corners into the mainstream.
  • Kindle Unlimited rewards series. Readers who finish Book 1 move straight on to Book 2, so later books earn more as the series grows. Single standalone books rarely build momentum.
  • Novellas give you speed. Shorter books let a series reach three instalments sooner. A visible series page on Amazon is one of the strongest signals for new readers.
  • The niche has to be specific. With competition rising, books win by matching an exact reader craving (a trope, a creature, a setting), not by being broadly "fantasy".

Reading Amazon rankings

Every Kindle book has a Best Sellers Rank (BSR). Independent estimates for the US Kindle store suggest roughly 1,000 sales a day at #100, about 13 a day at #10,000 and about 1 a day at #100,000. Holding a rank under 30,000 in a sub-niche is a solid result for a new author. Ranks move daily, and these conversions are estimates with a margin of error.

Monster Romance

Fantasy romance where the love interest is a non-human "monster" (orcs, demons, gargoyles, aliens). The tone is romantic and swoony, with a guaranteed happy ending.

Why it works

  • It is one of romance's breakout trends. It moved from self-published corners into the mainstream, and BookTok and Kindle Unlimited keep it going.
  • It has dedicated reader events, such as the Monster Romance "Stuff Your Kindle" day on 31 October 2026.
  • Indie authors lead it, so there is room for new names.
  • Novellas of 15,000–40,000 words are standard, so your book will meet reader expectations.

Target reader

Women aged 25–45 who read romantasy, are heavy KU users and binge several books a week. They look for tropes like arranged marriage, he falls first and grumpy/sunshine.

Cozy Romantasy

Romantic fantasy with a warm, low-stakes heart: magical shops, small villages, found family and a love story at the centre. It is the "comfort read" side of romantasy.

Why it works

  • Market analysts named cozy fantasy one of the hottest niches of 2026. It is still described as an emerging niche with demand that is not yet fully met.
  • It has its own Amazon bestseller category and its own reader promotions, such as the October 2026 cozy fantasy & romantasy events.
  • Book 1 of indie series (often 4–13 books long) appear in the category charts.

Target reader

Romantasy readers aged 25–55 who want comfort over carnage. They respond to cottagecore aesthetics, tea shops, bookshops and gentle magic.

Paranormal Cozy Mystery

A light, clean mystery with witches, ghosts or a talking familiar, plus a slow-burn romance running across the series. It is the most "bingeable" series format in Kindle Unlimited.

Why it works

  • It is an evergreen niche with very loyal readers who follow a series for 10 books or more.
  • The Kindle Unlimited category is full of long series and box sets, which shows that readers move from one book to the next.
  • Novellas of 15,000–40,000 words are common and accepted.

Target reader

Women aged 35–65 who are avid KU readers. They prefer clean content and love quirky towns, pets with personality and puzzles they can solve along with the heroine.
